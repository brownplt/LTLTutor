Be mindful of operator precedence Tip

It may be the case that you are unsure about the precedence and associativity rules of operators in LTL formulas, and realy on parentheses alone to resolve any ambiguities. However, this is a misconception that can lead to confusion and misinterpretation of formulas. LTL follows strict rules regarding operator precedence and associativity.
In the absence of parentheses, it is important to be wary of operator precedence and associativity rules.

  • Unary operators ({{ ltl_code('X p', operator_only=True) }}, {{ ltl_code('F p', operator_only=True) }}, {{ ltl_code('G p', operator_only=True) }}, {{ ltl_code('!p', operator_only=True) }}) have the highest precedence and will bind tightly to the formula that immediately follows them.
  • Binary operators ({{ ltl_code('p & q', operator_only=True) }},{{ ltl_code('p | q', operator_only=True) }},{{ ltl_code('p -> q', operator_only=True) }},{{ ltl_code('p <-> q', operator_only=True) }},{{ ltl_code('p U q', operator_only=True) }}) are left associative. This means that in expressions involving multiple operators, the operations are grouped from left to right.
    For instance, an expression like a | b & c -> d would be interpreted as ((a | b) & c) -> d.
