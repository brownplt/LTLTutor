Some learners mistakenly believe that the next ({{ ltl_code('X p', operator_only=True) }}) operator in LTL can apply to multiple states or spread its effect across a range of states. However, this is not true. The {{ ltl_code('X p', operator_only=True) }} operator is very precise: it applies strictly to the immediate next state and nothing else.

For example, the formula {{ ltl_code('X (X p)') }} means that {{ ltl_code('p') }} holds two states from now. It does not imply anything about {{ ltl_code('p') }} holding in the current state, the immediate next state, or any state beyond the two-step distance specified.

Another way to think of this is that the next operator does not fan out its effect; it's not about a condition holding over a range of states or eventually. It applies strictly to the very next state.
